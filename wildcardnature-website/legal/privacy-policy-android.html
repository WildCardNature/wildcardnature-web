Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 19, 2026 · Last updated: October 25, 2026

This policy applies to the Google Play edition of WildCard Nature for Android.

WildCard Nature ("we," "our," or "us") turns the wildlife photos you take into collectible, AI-stylized trading cards. This Privacy Policy explains what information the app collects, how it is used, who it is shared with, how long it is kept, and how you can delete it.

1. Who We Are and How to Contact Us

WildCard Nature is developed and operated by Jason Satz, which is responsible for the personal information described in this policy.

For privacy questions, requests, or account deletion, email tex@discvault.us.

2. Information We Collect

Two ways to play: Google sign-in or guest

You can sign in with Google, or tap Continue as guest on the sign-in screen and play without an account.

  • Google sign-in: we receive your Google name, email address, and account ID, as described below.
  • Guest mode: we do not receive your name or email address. Instead, the app creates a random anonymous user ID with Google Firebase Anonymous Authentication. That ID identifies your guest account, and the same game progress data described below is stored under it.

Keep your guest progress by signing in with Google. Your guest ID is stored only on your device. If you uninstall the app, clear its data, or sign out of the guest account, the ID is lost, and neither you nor we can link the guest's cloud progress back to you. To keep it, use Keep your progress in the app to sign in with Google while you are still playing as a guest (see "Upgrading a guest account" below). You can also export a backup file under Settings: a backup made as a guest can be restored into any account later, including a new guest account or a Google account.

Upgrading a guest account to Google sign-in

  • If you choose Keep your progress and sign in with Google, our server moves your guest's synced progress into your Google account's record, then deletes the guest record and the guest ID. From then on, your data is handled as described for Google sign-in, and we receive your Google name, email address, and account ID.
  • Cards and artwork stay on your device.

Personal info: name

  • Google sign-in only: your Google profile name. It becomes your in-app display name, which you can change under Profile → Edit Profile.
  • Guests: you start with the default display name "Explorer" followed by the last 4 characters of your guest ID (for example, "Explorer 7QX2"). You can change it under Profile → Edit Profile.
  • Your display name appears on the public leaderboard (see section 5).
  • Display names are checked when you set them, so that names containing web addresses, email addresses, or offensive words are rejected. This check uses only the name you type and collects no additional data.

Personal info: email address

  • Google sign-in only: the email address of the Google account you sign in with. It is used for account management, shown on your Profile, and stored on your device. Guests are never asked for an email address.

Personal info: user IDs

  • Google sign-in: your Google account ID. We use it as the key for your cloud-synced progress and your daily generation counter, and it identifies your leaderboard entry.
  • An anonymous Firebase user ID, created automatically for every player so the app can read and write the leaderboard. For guests, this ID is also the key for your cloud-synced progress, your daily generation counter, and your leaderboard entry.
  • A random in-app user ID generated on your device, used when counting weekly species discoveries.

Daily generation counter

  • Each player can create a limited number of free cards per day. To enforce this fairly, every card-creation request carries a sign-in token that our server checks to confirm which account is making it. The token itself is not stored.
  • For each account and day, our server stores a counter made of your account ID (your Google account ID, or your guest ID), your device's local date, and the number of cards created that day.
  • The counter is used only for fair use and abuse prevention. It is deleted automatically 3 days after it is created.
  • If you have bought generation packs (see "Purchases" below), a card you create after your free cards for the day uses one of your purchased generations.
  • Guests only: to stop the guest option from being used to get around the daily limit, our server also counts guest cards created from each network per day. The counter is keyed by a one-way hash of your IP address combined with a random secret value that changes every day; the raw IP address is not stored. The secret is deleted after 2 days, so old hashes cannot be linked back to an IP address, and the counter itself is deleted automatically 3 days after it is created. If many guests share one network (for example, a school or office Wi-Fi), they share this network limit; signing in with Google avoids it.

App integrity check (Google Play Integrity)

  • To protect our server from abuse by modified apps and automated scripts, the app uses Google Firebase App Check with the Google Play Integrity API. When the app contacts our server, Google Play services on your device sends information to Google so that Google can check that the request comes from the genuine WildCard Nature app on a genuine Android device. Google describes this information as app details (such as package name, version, and signing certificate), the app's Google Play license status, and device attestation data generated by Google Play services; see how the Play Integrity API handles data and the Firebase privacy information. Google handles this data under its own terms and says it does not transfer it to third parties.
  • Google then gives the app a short-lived, signed App Check token that the app sends to our server. Our server only checks that the token is valid and counts how many requests per day passed or failed the check. The token tells us which of our apps sent the request; it contains no name, email address, device identifier, or location, and we do not store it.

Location: approximate location

Approximate location only: The Google Play edition asks Android only for approximate location permission. It does not request precise (GPS-level) location, so Android gives the app only an approximate position, typically accurate to an area of about 3 square kilometers. The app then rounds those coordinates on your device to 2 decimal places before they are used, stored, or sent anywhere. The app never receives, stores, or shares your precise location.

  • The app asks for location only while you are using it (for example, when you capture a card or open the Field Guide). It never accesses location in the background.
  • Location is optional. If you deny location permission, the app still works, but cards will not have a habitat region and the Field Guide cannot detect your region automatically.
  • Rounded coordinates are stored with your cards on your device. They are used to place cards on the Discovery Map and to write each card's location lore.
  • To turn rounded coordinates into a region name (for example, a state or province), your device sends them directly to OpenStreetMap Nominatim.
  • Only the region name and country, not coordinates, are sent to our server to fetch the species list for your region.

Photos

  • Wildlife photos you take with the in-app camera. You cannot upload photos from your gallery.
  • To create card artwork, a resized copy of the photo goes through our server to Stability AI, which returns the stylized image. The resulting artwork and your cards are stored on your device.
  • We do not store your photos on our servers.
  • Photo metadata (date, time, camera model, and exposure settings) is read on your device and shown as card "lore." Any coordinates used for a card come from the approximate, rounded location described above.

Content reports

You can report card artwork that the AI produced, or a player on the leaderboard, from inside the app. When you send a report, our server stores:

  • what you reported (artwork or a player) and an identifier for it: the card's ID, or the reported player's leaderboard entry ID;
  • the reason you chose, and an optional note you write (up to 300 characters);
  • for artwork reports, the card's species, art style, and rarity, if included. The report does not include the artwork image or your photo;
  • the app edition and the time of the report; and
  • a reporter identifier, so we can spot abuse of the reporting tool. This is a one-way SHA-256 hash of your Google account ID (or, in guest mode, of your guest ID) after our server verifies your sign-in. If your sign-in cannot be verified, it is instead a hash of your IP address combined with a secret value that changes every day. Reports never store your name, email address, or raw IP address.

Do not include personal information in the optional note. If another player reports you, the report contains your leaderboard entry ID and the reason given, and is handled and retained the same way.

Hidden players (stays on your device)

  • You can hide a player on the leaderboard. Your list of hidden players is stored only on your device. It is never sent to our server, and the hidden player is not told.

App activity: in-app actions and other user-generated content

  • Your game progress, synced to our server: XP, level, streaks, achievements, Field Guide progress, in-game Field Tokens, daily free-generation usage, and, if you have bought generation packs, your purchased-generation balance. If you sign in with Google, it is restored when you reinstall and sign in again. Guest progress is stored the same way, but cannot be restored after the guest ID is lost unless you first moved it into a Google account with Keep your progress (see "Two ways to play" above).
  • Your XP and level, published on the leaderboard.
  • Species names you enter or confirm for your cards, plus card names you choose. These stay on your device, except for the species of a capture that matches the weekly featured species (see section 3).

Purchases (optional)

You can buy optional generation packs in the app. Each purchased generation lets you create one card beyond your free daily allowance. Buying requires Google sign-in; if you play as a guest, the app asks you to sign in with Google first.

Google Play handles your payment. Purchases are made through Google Play Billing. Your card, bank, or other payment details go to Google, not to us: neither the app nor our server ever receives them.

  • RevenueCat: the app uses RevenueCat, Inc. as our purchase-processing service provider. RevenueCat verifies each purchase with Google Play and notifies our server. The RevenueCat software in the app sends RevenueCat:
    • your purchase account ID: "google:" followed by your Google account ID. The app starts RevenueCat only after you sign in with Google, so guests and signed-out players never contact RevenueCat;
    • for each purchase, the Google Play purchase token (receipt) and transaction details: the product, price, currency, and time of purchase; and
    • basic technical information the software needs to work, such as your locale and store currency.
    The app does not send RevenueCat your name, email address, or advertising ID, and does not turn on RevenueCat's optional collection of device identifiers. RevenueCat states that it does not collect location data (other than locale and currency code), crash logs, or diagnostics.
  • Our server: when RevenueCat confirms a purchase, our server adds the generations to your account's cloud record and stores a purchase history entry: the product, the number of generations, the Google Play transaction ID, whether it was a test or real purchase, the purchase time, and whether it was refunded.
  • Duplicate protection: our server also keeps a separate record for each transaction, keyed by its Google Play transaction ID, so that a purchase is never credited twice and refunds are handled correctly. It contains the product, the number of generations, whether it was a test or real purchase, its credit and refund status, and a one-way SHA-256 hash of your Google account ID. It does not contain your Google account ID itself, your name, or your email address.
  • Refunds: when Google Play refunds a purchase, our server removes that purchase's generations from your balance and marks the history entry as refunded. Generations you have already used cannot be removed, so we record the difference.

What we do not collect

  • We do not collect app performance data or crash logs, advertising or device identifiers, payment information such as card or bank details, messages, contacts, health data, or web browsing history. We do keep the purchase history described under "Purchases" above.
  • The app contains no advertising and no analytics or crash-reporting tools.

3. How We Use Your Information

  • Account management: to sign you in with Google or as a guest, keep you signed in, link your progress to your account, and move a guest's progress into a Google account when you choose Keep your progress.
  • Card generation: to turn your photo into stylized card artwork.
  • Regional features: to identify your region from approximate location, show the species that live there, and feature a regional "Species of the Week."
  • Weekly discovery counter: when you capture the featured species, we record one discovery for your region and week, using your in-app user ID so you are counted only once.
  • Progress sync: to save XP, streaks, achievements, and Field Guide progress, and restore them on a new install.
  • Leaderboard: to rank players by XP.
  • Safety and moderation: to review reports of offensive AI-generated artwork or inappropriate player names, act on them (for example, removing or resetting a display name, or improving our content filters), and detect misuse of the reporting tool.
  • Fair use and abuse prevention: our server keeps the daily generation counter described in section 2 so each account gets its fair share of free cards, plus the per-network guest counter (keyed by a daily-salted hash of your IP address). It also counts requests per IP address to enforce rate limits. The IP rate-limit counters expire automatically within one hour.
  • Security: to confirm, through Google Play Integrity and Firebase App Check, that requests to our server come from the genuine app (see section 2).
  • Display name checks: to reject names that contain web addresses, email addresses, or offensive words.
  • Purchases: to process generation pack purchases, verify them with Google Play through RevenueCat, add the generations to your account, keep your purchase history, remove generations when a purchase is refunded, and prevent duplicate credits and purchase fraud.

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not use it for advertising.

5. Public Leaderboard

The leaderboard is visible to all players of WildCard Nature, including guests. Your entry shows your display name, your chosen avatar (one of the app's preset avatars, not your Google photo), your XP, and your level. The entry is stored under an internal account identifier.

If you do not want your Google name to appear, change your display name under Profile → Edit Profile before you open the leaderboard. Guests appear as "Explorer" plus the last 4 characters of their guest ID until they choose another name.

If another player's name is offensive, you can report the player (see "Content reports" in section 2) or hide them, which removes them from the leaderboard on your device only. Display names must follow our Terms of Service; we may remove or reset names that break them.

Deleting your account removes your leaderboard entry (see section 7).

6. Data Retention

  • On your device: cards, artwork, photos, rounded locations, your profile, your hidden-players list, and sign-in tokens stay on your device until you delete your account, clear the app's data, or uninstall the app. Uninstalling removes them unless you exported a backup file yourself.
  • Progress sync (Cloudflare Workers storage): kept while your account exists, and deleted when you delete your account. This includes guest accounts. If you uninstall or clear the app without deleting your guest account first, its progress record stays on our server, but it is linked only to a random ID that we cannot connect to you.
  • Purchase history and purchased generations (Cloudflare Workers storage): part of your account's cloud record. Kept while your account exists (the history keeps your most recent 100 purchases), and deleted when you delete your account, together with any purchased generations you have not used.
  • Duplicate-protection records for purchases: deleted automatically 400 days after the purchase or refund was last processed, so that late refunds are still handled correctly. They are not deleted when you delete your account, but they contain only a one-way hash of your Google account ID, not the ID itself.
  • Google Play and RevenueCat: keep records of completed purchases under their own privacy policies and to meet their legal, tax, and accounting obligations. Deleting your account in the app does not delete them. To ask us to request deletion of your RevenueCat customer record, email tex@discvault.us (see section 7).
  • Leaderboard (Google Firebase): kept while your account exists, and deleted when you delete your account. The anonymous Firebase user is also deleted.
  • Daily generation counters: deleted automatically 3 days after they are created, including after you delete your account. This includes the per-network guest counters; the daily secret used to hash IP addresses is deleted after 2 days.
  • App Check totals: daily counts of requests that passed or failed the integrity check, with no account or device information, deleted automatically after 14 days.
  • Guest record after an upgrade: deleted when its progress is moved into your Google account.
  • Weekly discovery records: expire automatically at the end of the featured week, always within 7 days.
  • Rate-limit counters: keyed by IP address and expire automatically within one hour.
  • Content reports: kept for up to 90 days so we can review them for safety and moderation, then deleted automatically. Because reports are needed to deal with abuse, deleting your account does not delete reports you sent before they expire. They contain only the hashed reporter identifier described in section 2, not your name or email address.
  • Stability AI: photos sent for stylization are handled under Stability AI's terms. Its privacy policy states that it keeps personal data "for as long as reasonably necessary." It may use API inputs and outputs to improve its models unless the account holder opts out.
  • Anthropic: receives only region names. Under its commercial terms, Anthropic does not train on API data by default and deletes API inputs and outputs within 30 days, with limited exceptions.
  • Airtable: keeps regional species lists indefinitely as a shared cache. They contain no personal data.
  • OpenStreetMap Nominatim: handles request logs under the OpenStreetMap Foundation's privacy policy.

7. Your Rights and Account Deletion

Delete your account in the app

  1. Open your Profile.
  2. Tap the Settings (gear) icon.
  3. Under Account, choose Delete account and confirm.

This deletes your synced progress from our server, your leaderboard entry, and your anonymous Firebase user. If you signed in with Google, it also revokes the app's access to your Google account. It then erases the app's data on your device, including your hidden-players list. Guests use the same steps, with no Google step. Content reports you sent are kept until they expire, up to 90 days, and daily generation counters expire within 3 days (see section 6).

Unused purchased generations are lost when you delete your account. Deleting your account also deletes your purchase history and any purchased generations you have not used. They cannot be restored, and deleting your account does not refund them.

Records of completed purchases held by Google Play and RevenueCat are not deleted by the app (see section 6). To ask us to request deletion of your RevenueCat customer record, email tex@discvault.us from the Google account you bought with.

Delete your account without the app

If you no longer have the app installed, follow the instructions at WildCard Nature account deletion. If you signed in with Google, you can also email tex@discvault.us from that Google account. We process requests within 30 days.

Guests: because guest accounts have no name or email address, and the guest ID is stored only on your device, we cannot find a guest's data once the app has been uninstalled or cleared. If you play as a guest, please delete your account in the app before you uninstall.

Other rights

  • Access and correction: you can view and edit your profile in the app, or email us for a copy of the data linked to your account.
  • Location: you can deny or revoke location permission in your device settings at any time.
  • Backup: you can export your card collection to a file under Settings. Backups made as a guest can be restored into any account.
  • Regional rights: depending on where you live (for example, in the EU, the UK, or California), you may have additional rights, such as objecting to processing or lodging a complaint with a data protection authority. Contact us to exercise them.

8. Children's Privacy

WildCard Nature is intended for users aged 13 and older. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal information, contact us at tex@discvault.us and we will delete it.

9. Security

  • All network traffic uses HTTPS. The app blocks unencrypted (cleartext) connections.
  • Sign-in tokens are stored in the device's secure, encrypted keystore.
  • The app uses approximate location only, and rounds it on your device before it is used or shared.
  • Content reports identify the reporter only by a one-way hash, never by name, email address, or raw IP address.
  • API keys for our service providers stay on our server and are never included in the app.
  • Payments are handled by Google Play; your payment details never reach the app or our server. Purchased generations are added only by our server after the purchase is verified, never by the app itself.

No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, but we work to protect your information.
